# Ducks Burger Bar — Website Pricing Options

## Option A — Flat Buyout (recommended lead offer)
**$3,200 one-time**
- Full ownership of code, design, and content on signing
- Hosting/domain stay in Client's name (or transferred to their account)
- 2 rounds of revisions included
- No ongoing commitment from either side

## Option B — Lower Entry + Monthly Care Plan
**$1,800 upfront + $125/month**
- Same site, lower buy-in
- Monthly plan covers hosting, domain renewal, uptime monitoring, and up to 2 content edits/month (menu items, hours, prices, seasonal promos)
- 6-month minimum, cancel anytime after with 30 days' notice
- Better for an owner who wants to stay hands-off and call you when something needs to change

## Option C — Tiered Packages (use if he wants to negotiate scope)
| | Basic | Standard | Premium |

| Current 5-page site | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

## Why this range
Comparable freelance/small-agency pricing for a *custom-coded* (not template/Wix/Squarespace) small-business site:
- DIY template sites: $500–$1,500 — not what was built here
- Custom small-business site, 4–6 pages, animations, working forms: **$2,500–$6,000**
- Full agency build with CMS/booking/ordering integration: $6,000+

Ducks' site lands solidly in the custom-build tier: hand-coded Next.js, custom animated components, a "Unlimited" membership promo page (a real revenue driver, not just a menu page), real photo/video assets, click-to-call/click-to-email contact links, already live and deployed. That work is done — you're pricing the finished asset, not a future estimate, so there's no reason to discount toward "hobbyist" rates.

**Recommendation:** open with Option A at $3,200. If he hesitates on the number, offer Option B — it lowers his upfront cash outlay while getting you a recurring line ($125/mo ≈ $1,500/yr) instead of a discount. Avoid dropping the one-time price below ~$2,200; below that you're pricing custom dev work like a $30 template.

**One scope note to flag with him directly:** the menu is currently hardcoded in the codebase, not in an editable CMS. If he expects to update prices/items himself, either price in the small CMS build (Option C Premium) or make clear that edits go through you (the retainer in Option B/C).
